    Component Selection

    Team 101: Automatic Pet Food Dispenser

    Student: Connor

    Subsystem: Weight/Height Sensor

    Project Overview

    The Weight/Height Sensor subsystem measures the weight of pet food dispensed into a bowl.

    The system uses a load cell, an MCP6004 operational amplifier, and a regulated 5V power supply to provide an analog signal to the PIC18F57Q43 microcontroller.

    1. Load Cell Sensor

    The load cell measures the weight of food dispensed into the pet's bowl. Three possible solutions were evaluated.

    Solution Pros Cons
    Option 1: SparkFun TAL220B, 5kg ($15.50) View Product 5kg capacity; four-wire Wheatstone bridge; IP65 protection; documented mounting dimensions. Higher cost; requires amplification; requires calibration and mechanical mounting.
    Option 2: SparkFun TAL220, 10kg ($12.95) View Product Higher weight capacity; documented specifications; IP66 protection; suitable for heavier bowls. Lower sensitivity for small portions at similar rated output; requires amplification; mechanical mounting required.
    Option 3: Adafruit 5kg Load Cell, 4541 ($3.95) View Product Low cost; compact design; 5kg capacity; four-wire sensor. Requires amplification; needs calibration; environmental protection requires verification.

    Selected Solution

    Option 1: SparkFun TAL220B, 5kg

    Selection Rationale

    The SparkFun TAL220B was selected because its 5kg capacity provides an appropriate starting measurement range for the automatic pet food dispenser.

    Its documented specifications and mounting dimensions support integration into the mechanical design.

    The sensor produces a differential signal that can be amplified by the MCP6004 before measurement by the PIC microcontroller.

    The final selection will be verified against the maximum expected bowl weight and required measurement resolution.

    2. Operational Amplifier

    The operational amplifier conditions the small differential voltage generated by the load cell before it is measured by the microcontroller.

    Three quad operational amplifiers were compared.

    Solution Pros Cons
    Option 1: Microchip MCP6004-I/P ($0.60) View Product Four amplifiers in one IC; operates from 1.8V to 6V; rail-to-rail input and output; through-hole DIP-14 package; low input bias current. Input offset voltage can affect precision; requires external feedback resistors; gain and calibration must be verified.
    Option 2: Texas Instruments LM324N ($0.58) View Product Four amplifier channels; inexpensive; through-hole DIP-14 package; operates from a single 5V supply. Not rail-to-rail; higher input offset and bias current; less suitable for signals approaching the positive supply rail.
    Option 3: Texas Instruments TLV9004IPWR ($0.85) View Product Four amplifier channels; rail-to-rail input and output; low power consumption; operates from 1.8V to 5.5V; relatively low typical offset voltage. Surface-mount TSSOP package is more difficult to solder; requires external resistors; smaller package complicates prototyping.

    Selected Solution

    Option 1: Microchip MCP6004-I/P

    Selection Rationale

    The MCP6004 was selected because it provides four operational amplifier channels in one integrated circuit and operates from the regulated 5V power supply.

    Three channels can be configured as a differential instrumentation amplifier for the load-cell sensor.

    The through-hole DIP-14 package simplifies prototyping, assembly, and troubleshooting compared with the smaller surface-mount TLV9004 package.

    The rail-to-rail input and output capability is also beneficial for operation with a single 5V supply.

    The design uses external feedback resistors to establish the required amplifier gain.

    Before final hardware assembly, the amplifier gain, input offset error, common-mode range, and output voltage must be verified against the selected load cell and PIC ADC.

    3. Linear Voltage Regulator

    The Weight Sensor subsystem requires a regulated 5V supply for the load cell and operational amplifier.

    The design uses a 9V DC external supply and a linear regulator to produce 5V.

    Voltage Regulator Comparison

    Solution Pros Cons
    Option 1: onsemi MC7805CTG ($0.99) View Product Fixed 5V output; rated output current of 1A; TO-220 through-hole package; internal thermal and short-circuit protection; easy to integrate with existing schematic. Dissipates heat when reducing 9V to 5V; requires bypass capacitors; not as efficient as a switching regulator.
    Option 2: STMicroelectronics L7805CV ($1.08) View Product Fixed 5V output; rated up to 1.5A with appropriate cooling; TO-220 package; thermal and short-circuit protection; widely available. Produces heat during voltage conversion; requires bypass capacitors; increased current capability is unnecessary for a low-power sensor circuit.
    Option 3: Texas Instruments LM340AT-5.0/NOPB ($1.97) View Product Regulated 5V output; 1.5A output rating with adequate thermal management; through-hole package; overcurrent and short-circuit protection. Higher cost; generates heat; requires additional capacitors and thermal analysis.

    Selected Solution

    Option 1: onsemi MC7805CTG

    Selection Rationale

    The MC7805CTG was selected because it provides a regulated 5V output using the available 9V DC input.

    Its TO-220 package is suitable for through-hole assembly and is compatible with the proposed linear regulator circuit.

    The 1A rated output current provides a useful design ceiling for the sensor electronics, although actual usable current is limited by thermal dissipation.

    The selected regulator also provides internal thermal protection and short-circuit protection.

    Compared with the alternative regulators, the MC7805CTG offers an appropriate combination of simplicity, cost, package size, and output requirements.

    Supporting Circuit Components

    Component Value Purpose
    U1 MC7805CTG Converts 9V DC to 5V DC
    C1 0.33uF Input bypass capacitor
    C2 0.1uF Output bypass capacitor
    J1 DC barrel jack External 9V power connection
    PWR_FLAG KiCad power flag Identifies externally supplied power

    The capacitor values are based on a standard 7805 regulator application circuit.

    Regulator Power Dissipation

    Linear regulators dissipate the voltage difference between their input and output as heat.

    Power dissipation can be estimated using:

    P = (Vin - Vout) x Iout

    At a 9V input and 5V output:

    P = (9 - 5) x Iout

    At a hypothetical 100mA load:

    P = 4 x 0.100 = 0.40W

    At a hypothetical 250mA load:

    P = 4 x 0.250 = 1.00W

    These examples do not represent measured system current.

    The regulator's actual power dissipation and required thermal management will be evaluated using the completed power budget.

    4. External Power Supply

    The subsystem requires an external DC power source to supply the voltage regulator.

    A 9V DC wall adapter was selected as the proposed power source.

    The 9V adapter output connects to the barrel jack and then to the input of the MC7805CTG.

    External Power Supply Comparison

    Solution Pros Cons
    Option 1: Adafruit 9V 1A Adapter, Product 63 ($8.95) View Product Regulated 9V output; 1A current rating; center-positive 5.5mm x 2.1mm barrel connector; UL listed; compact wall adapter; documented specifications. Limited to 1A; requires an appropriate PCB barrel jack; additional voltage regulation is required for 5V electronics.
    Option 2: Pololu 1470, 9V 1A Adapter ($11.41) View Product Regulated 9V output; 1A rating; center-positive 5.5mm x 2.1mm barrel connector; 100-240V AC input; documented specifications. Higher cost than Option 1; requires a matching barrel jack; maximum current is limited to 1A.
    Option 3: Tensility 16-00138, 9V 2A Adapter ($10.80) View Product 9V output; 2A current rating; barrel connector; UL-related approval; higher supply-current capacity for possible expansion. Extra current capacity is unnecessary for the sensor alone; regulator heating still limits the 5V rail; connector compatibility must be verified.

    Selected Solution

    Option 1: Adafruit 9V 1A Power Adapter

    Selection Rationale

    The Adafruit 9V 1A adapter was selected because it provides a regulated 9V DC output suitable for the input of the MC7805CTG regulator.

    Its center-positive barrel connector supports a common 5.5mm x 2.1mm power connection.

    The 1A current rating provides sufficient nominal capacity for the proposed low-power sensor electronics, subject to confirmation through the final power budget.

    The adapter is compact, commercially available, and supported by published product specifications.

    The selected supply also avoids the complexity of battery charging or battery replacement.

    The final design must use a barrel jack with matching dimensions, pinout, and polarity.

    5. Barrel Jack Connection

    The barrel jack provides the electrical connection between the external wall adapter and the PCB.

    The proposed jack accepts a 5.5mm outer diameter and 2.1mm inner diameter plug.

    The design uses a center-positive connection.

    Proposed Connections

    Connection Function
    Center contact +9V DC input
    Outer contact Ground
    Switched contact Unused
    Regulator input +9V DC
    Regulator output +5V DC

    The schematic uses a switched barrel jack symbol with the unused switched contact marked as no-connect.

    Before PCB fabrication, the specific jack part number, pin numbering, and footprint must be checked against its manufacturer datasheet.

    A barrel jack is treated as an electrical connector rather than a major active component for the component-selection tables.

    6. Signal Conditioning Circuit

    The load cell produces a differential voltage that is significantly smaller than the microcontroller's ADC input range.

    The MCP6004 is configured using three operational amplifiers.

    First Amplification Stage

    Amplifier U2A receives the positive load-cell signal.

    Amplifier U2B receives the negative load-cell signal.

    Each amplifier uses a 10k feedback resistor.

    A 1k resistor connects the two inverting-input feedback nodes.

    The ideal differential gain of the first stage is:

    Gain = 1 + (2 x R) / Rg

    Where:

    R = 10,000 ohms

    Rg = 1,000 ohms

    Gain = 1 + (20,000 / 1,000)

    First-stage gain = 21

    Differential Amplifier Stage

    Amplifier U2C subtracts the first-stage amplifier outputs.

    Four 10k resistors establish a nominal unity-gain difference amplifier.

    For the intended resistor arrangement:

    Vout = OUT_A - OUT_B

    Combined Amplifier Gain

    The intended nominal total differential gain is approximately 21.

    The final gain must be verified using the selected load cell's rated sensitivity.

    The design must also account for input offset voltage, resistor tolerance, common-mode voltage, supply headroom, and ADC input voltage limits.

    For accurate low-weight measurements, additional gain, offset correction, or a dedicated load-cell amplifier may be required.

    Unused Amplifier Channel

    The fourth MCP6004 amplifier channel, U2D, is configured as a voltage follower with its non-inverting input connected to ground.

    This prevents the unused amplifier inputs from floating.

    Microcontroller Interface

    The conditioned weight signal is intended to connect to an analog input on the PIC18F57Q43 Curiosity Nano board.

    The block diagram proposes RA0.

    The final ADC channel assignment, input-voltage limits, and board power connections require verification using the Microchip documentation.

    8. Power Budget Planning

    The electrical design uses two voltages:

    • 9V from the external wall adapter
    • 5V from the linear voltage regulator

    The load cell and MCP6004 operate from the regulated 5V rail.

    The microcontroller supply arrangement must be confirmed before final assembly.

    Components Requiring Power

    Component Supply Voltage Power Budget Status
    Load cell 5V excitation Current to be calculated from bridge resistance
    MCP6004 5V Maximum current to be obtained from datasheet
    PIC18F57Q43 Curiosity Nano Board-dependent 5V power arrangement Maximum current to be verified
    LED indicator Design dependent Current to be determined by LED and resistor
    MC7805CTG 9V input, 5V output Regulator efficiency and heating to be calculated

    The voltage regulator and external power source are included in the appropriate power-budget sections rather than being treated as ordinary 5V loads.

    The final power budget will include maximum operating currents and a 25% current safety margin.

    The external supply and voltage regulator must both support the total required current.

    10. Design Summary

    The Weight Sensor subsystem uses a strain-gauge load cell to measure the amount of pet food dispensed.

    A three-amplifier signal conditioning circuit is implemented using the MCP6004 operational amplifier.

    The conditioned analog output is intended to be measured by the PIC18F57Q43 microcontroller.

    The proposed circuit uses an external 9V DC wall adapter and an MC7805CTG linear voltage regulator to provide a regulated 5V supply.

    The selected components provide a practical starting design using commercially available parts with published technical specifications.

    Before physical implementation, the design must be validated through power-budget calculations, electrical simulation or testing, sensor calibration, thermal analysis, and verification of PCB footprints.

    The schematic has been checked using KiCad Electrical Rules Check. A successful ERC verifies connectivity rules but does not establish that the analog circuit meets its performance requirements.
